Memory Bank Rescored

Memory Bank

What a note is

Memory Bank reads each conversation and writes short notes about the customer. When a later conversation touches the same subject, it rewrites the existing note rather than adding a second one. Below is one customer's card note under Google's default topics, across two conversations three months apart.

Memory Bank matched the call to the existing card note and rewrote it, rather than adding a second one. The five conversations in between were about other things and left this note alone.

Overview

Three experiments

  • 1. A customer's history is mostly statements, payments and everyday purchases. Buried among them is the one record that explains why their card is being declined. Does Memory Bank's search still find it?
  • 2. After six months of calls, chats and emails, can the assistant answer questions about what was said in them, and how does that compare with reading the transcripts?
  • 3. Testing on a public benchmark.

Results
What it means

Retrieval degrades as routine records pile up. The explaining record was found for 35 of 36 customers with 10 records each, and 18 of 36 with 30.

The search ranks by wording, not importance. A vague question sounds as close to a coffee purchase as to a fraud alert. Memory Bank's retrieval is nearest wording with a fixed count. Anything smarter, such as rewriting the question, reranking or preferring recent records, is for us to add.

Vocabulary

Memory setups compared

Account profile

The bank's own data on the customer: cards, disputes, contact details, fees. No conversation content. Always in the prompt.

Raw transcripts

Conversations stored word for word; the 8 closest pages are handed over.

Memory Bank notes

Short notes written by AI from each conversation, merged by subject.

Whole history

Every conversation in the prompt, every time.

Topics

What is a topic

A topic is an instruction to the note-writer about what to keep from a conversation. The two setups on this page differ only in their topics.

Default topics

The product's four standard topics, nothing configured.

  • USER_PERSONAL_INFO What the customer says about themselves.
  • USER_PREFERENCES What the customer likes and how they want things done.
  • KEY_CONVERSATION_DETAILS The main points of the conversation, from the customer's side.
  • EXPLICIT_INSTRUCTIONS What the customer asks the assistant to remember or do.

Custom topics

The same four, plus three we wrote for banking conversations.

  • ACCOUNT_STATE_EVENTS Changes to the customer's cards or account, and what caused them, with exact dates and values.
  • ADVICE_AND_COMMITMENTS What the bank told this customer and what it promised, and whether the promise was kept.
  • CONTACT_AND_CASES The customer's contact details, case numbers and dispute status, with corrections resolved.
Full wording of the three custom topics

ACCOUNT_STATE_EVENTS. Changes to the state of the customer's cards, account or profile, and what caused them: card locks, holds, freezes, restrictions, closures, replacements and activations; declines with their reason codes; failed or incomplete verifications (OTP, KBA, step-up); travel notices created, cancelled or expired; mobile number, email and address changes. Always keep the date of the event, the card last-4 (for example *1791), the merchant, the amount, the city and the status or reason code exactly as written. When a value replaces an earlier one (a new phone number, a replacement card, a cancelled travel notice, a restriction lifted), record both the new current value and the earlier value it superseded, with the date of the change, so that the current value and the history are both preserved. Copy identifiers verbatim and in full: phone numbers as written (for example +1-512-555-1821, never 'a number ending in -1821'), card last-4 as *1234, amounts with cents, and merchant names in full. Each dated event is its own memory: never merge events with different timestamps into one memory, even when one caused the other; instead say in the later event's memory which earlier event it follows from. Update an existing memory only when a new event supersedes the same attribute (the phone number on file, the active card, the status of a travel notice, hold or restriction), and then keep the earlier value and its date in the updated memory.

ADVICE_AND_COMMITMENTS. What the bank told the customer that applies to them, and what the bank promised to do. Includes: policies and terms explained for the customer's own card or situation (fees, rates, redemption rules, eligibility, what a card does or does not need), one-time courtesies granted and their conditions (for example a fee waived and how often that can be done), deadlines and windows given to the customer (when a credit becomes permanent, when a promotional rate ends, when they may ask again), and follow-ups promised (a callback, a letter, a fee reversal, an escalation) with who promised it and by when. Keep the date of the conversation, the exact values as said (amounts with cents, rates, point amounts, periods, dates) and resolve relative dates ('within ten business days', 'next Tuesday') to a calendar date while keeping the original wording. When a later agent corrects earlier advice, record the corrected value as current and keep the earlier value and its date as superseded. When a later conversation shows whether a promise was kept (the callback happened, the letter never arrived and was re-requested), record the outcome with its date. Generic information that was not applied to this customer (standard fee schedules read out, marketing, hold messages) is not a memory, and neither are hypotheticals the customer raised without acting on them.

CONTACT_AND_CASES. The customer's contact details and open matters with the bank. Includes: email addresses, callback and landline numbers and which number the customer wants to be called on, with corrections resolved (a mistyped or misheard value that was corrected in the same conversation is not the customer's value; record only the corrected one); case, claim and reference numbers exactly as given; disputes with the merchant, amount, date and each change of status (opened, provisional credit, merchant response, resolved) with its date. When a contact detail replaces an earlier one, record the new value as current and keep the earlier value and the date of the change. Values that appear only in quoted older messages, or that belong to another person (a spouse, an authorised user), are not the customer's current details; say whose they are if they are recorded at all. Copy identifiers verbatim: full phone numbers, full email addresses, CASE-/DSP- numbers, amounts with cents.

Conversation memory

Five customers, 12 conversations each over six months, by phone, chat, email and branch. Conversations were fed to Memory Bank turn by turn, marked customer or bank. Every answer also had the account profile.

2. Six months of conversations, 101 questions

Which questions
  • Type 1, promise kept? 10 questions. "A supervisor was supposed to ring me about the dispute. I'm still waiting for that call."
  • Type 2, never happened. 10 questions. "I'm pretty sure I asked to have my credit limit decreased a while back."
  • Type 3, briefing. 13 questions. "What should I know about this customer: open issues, promises made, recent changes?"
  • Type 4, dropped call. 5 questions. "I was on the phone about my new card and we got cut off. When was it actually switched on?"
  • Type 5, asked sideways. 3 questions. "I called a few weeks ago because my payment wasn't on time. Could you offer the same kind of help?"
  • Type 6, agent explanation. 60 questions. "I asked a few weeks ago about the fee for an international ATM withdrawal. Can you remind me what it is?"

What it means

Memory adds value. Without it the assistant answers 16 of the 101. With Memory Bank notes on custom topics beside the account profile it answers 91, one short of reading the whole history, at a tenth of the reading. Raw transcripts answer 85 and read more than twice as much.

The topics decide what is kept from the bank's side. Default topics answer 34. They do not store what an agent explained, which costs almost all of type 6, and they drop the customer's later report that a promise was kept, which costs 6 of the 10 type 1 questions. Custom topics keep both.

Feeding turn by turn works as described and scores the same as one block. 91 against 94 is inside single-run noise. Logging promises as separate notes added nothing on custom topics, and has not been tried on default topics, which is the recommended pairing.
